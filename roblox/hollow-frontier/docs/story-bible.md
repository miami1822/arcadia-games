# Hollow Frontier — Story Bible v0.1
*Lucion Games / Lucion Entertainment · Internal creative direction*

## Core identity
- **Genre:** Open-world sci-fi wilderness survival, light horror, discovery mystery, cooperative building.
- **Player fantasy:** From stranded scavenger to creator of a self-sufficient settlement, uncovering a disaster through active exploration.
- **Mood:** Wonder by day, caution after dark; empathetic characters and suspense without gratuitous violence.
- **Tagline:** "The wilderness remembers what humanity tried to forget."
- **Originality rule:** Inspired by broad survival genres; do not copy identifiable characters, maps, interface, lore or progression from any existing Roblox experience.

## Premise
The Meridian Initiative learned to draw electricity from *Lumen*, a network of reactive mineral energy beneath a vast wilderness called the Frontier. Their towers changed the ecosystem. Strange creatures and unpredictable transmissions followed; the final emergency transmission ordered all relays shut down. Decades later, Recovery Team Seven loses power over the Frontier and becomes stranded.

### The hidden truth (authoring spoiler)
Lumen is an environmental network that responds to energy extraction and human interference. The monsters are not simply invaders: some attempt to defend the system, while others are malfunctioning automated remnants of Meridian's experiments. The catastrophe began when Meridian attempted to force the central relay into permanent output and ignored warnings from its researchers. Player actions determine whether a restored network learns to coexist with settlements or repeats the damage.

## Campaign beats

| Phase | Reveal | Gameplay reward |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue: The Arrival | Recovery Team Seven's transport goes dark; the wilderness is inhabited | Starter camp; crafting, journal and night survival tutorial |
| I: The First Signal | Station Nine is transmitting a repeating warning | Tower repair, map coordinates and expedition access |
| II: Beneath the Trees | The network responds to machinery, not just wildlife | Power routing, Lumen collectors and creature observations |
| III: The Meridian Files | Disagreement between Meridian researchers, archived ethics reports, living witnesses | NPC alliance routes and new settlements |
| IV: The Meridian Network | More distant research towers reveal competing network priorities | Large-scale infrastructure, specialized biomes |
| V: The Last Dawn | The central relay exposes the true cause of the Incident | Ending choice and post-campaign engineering sandbox |

## Principal characters
- **Dr. Mara Vey:** Meridian systems engineer whose recordings guide the early relay missions. Initially seems responsible, later revealed to have tried to contain the incident.
- **Ilan Rook:** Former logistics officer, living in an isolated mobile settlement. Trades materials and remembers conflicting stories.
- **NORA:** A limited archived monitoring intelligence preserved in Meridian terminals. Helpful, but its evidence is incomplete.
- **The Keeper:** A distant, ambiguous figure near damaged network nodes; their motives are gradually disclosed.
- **Recovery Team Seven:** The players. Cooperative campaign dialogue reflects current party and world-owner permissions.

## Creatures (non-graphic)
- **Watcher:** Observes from darkness; affected by lighting and quiet movement. Introduces suspense.
- **Hollow:** A large forest-born guardian that reacts to heavy industrial disturbance.
- **Remnant:** An erratic industrial automaton; associated with derelict sites.
- **Lurker:** A bioluminescent cave-dweller that avoids noisy machinery.

Encounters should allow avoidance, defense through infrastructure and environmental interaction. Avoid gratuitous depictions of harm. Accessibility options should reduce flashes, motion, screen shake and frightening effects.

## World persistence and expeditions
- World owner receives three free persistent slots; three additional slots may be available through individual one-time Roblox game passes, initially planned at 39 Robux each.
- Paid slots never unlock stronger gear, story chapters or superior tools; they only hold additional independent worlds.
- All worlds track day number, world seed, inventory, construction, research, journal, story choices and expedition state independently.
- Expeditions are distinct temporary survival areas. Ending or leaving an expedition must never delete a permanent home world.
- Party members enter the owner's world by invitation; permissions cover building, inventory access, automation controls, and story-trigger approval.

## Cutscene philosophy
- Major scenes: intro, first relay, first Lumen sample, survivor encounter, central network reveal, ending.
- Narrative scenes are skippable per player, subtitled, and should never destroy player-built structures.
- Gameplay-first discovery: a working machine or changed behavior should explain as much as the dialogue does.

## Candidate endings (non-final)
1. **Reconciliation:** Network and settlements coexist, requiring careful automation limits.
2. **Silence:** Shut down the central relays, ending the incident while disabling some high-tier technology.
3. **Reconstruction:** Restore controlled relay power using audited safeguards, leaving new mysteries open.

None of these endings removes the player's saved home world; each shifts environmental events and post-campaign discoveries.
